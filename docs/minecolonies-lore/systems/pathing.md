Colonists are smarter pathfinders than standard villagers. They navigate flat ground, single-block steps, stairs, slabs, and similar surfaces without difficulty. They know how to open wooden and iron doors, trapdoors, and fence gates without any redstone input, and they climb ladders and vines. They actively avoid fences and route around hazards like fire, lava, magma, cacti, and sweet berry bushes.

If a colonist becomes trapped — falls into a hole, gets walled in by a Builder mid-construction, or cannot find any valid route — they will wait and then eventually forcibly relocate themselves to a nearby valid position and continue on their way. This recovery takes time and occasionally leads to a guard wandering somewhere dangerous, but it is rare.

Colonists move faster on path blocks and prefer them when navigating, so building roads out of these blocks helps keep colonists safe and moving efficiently. Waypoints complement this by guiding long-distance travel — placing one on a bridge or in a difficult-to-navigate area helps colonists choose better routes across the colony. Only a handful of waypoints are needed. The Builder can construct them with the Scepter of Gold, and several decorative path schematics already include waypoints as part of their design.

Research at the [[University]] can improve colonist movement speed. The Rails research in particular lets colonists use rail tracks — they create their own minecart automatically, so only the rails need to be placed. Powered rails add extra speed on hills but are not required. Rail intersections should include a short gap of a block or two so colonists do not overshoot turns.

Colonists can float on water surfaces the same way players do, but they cannot use boats and will drown if left in deep water too long.

A village is inhabited by [villagers](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Villager \"Villager\")\n, [cats](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Cat \"Cat\")\n, [iron golems](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Iron_golem \"Iron golem\")\n and livestock mobs. [Wandering traders](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Wandering_trader \"Wandering trader\")\n with their [trader llamas](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Trader_llama \"Trader llama\")\n can occasionally spawn there.\n\nVillages are a source of resources for the player, obtained through trading, loot chests, and other materials found within the village. They are also targeted by [illager raids](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Raid \"Raid\")\n, which are triggered when a player with the [Bad Omen](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Bad_Omen \"Bad Omen\")\n effect enters a village.\n\nGeneration\n----------\n\n[](https://minecraft.wiki/w/File:Village_Vibrant_Visuals.png)\n\nA visual of the [plains](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Plains \"Plains\")\n village\n\nVillages generate naturally in [plains](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Plains \"Plains\")\n, [savanna](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Savanna \"Savanna\")\n, [taiga](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Taiga \"Taiga\")\n, [meadows](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Meadow \"Meadow\")\n, [snowy plains](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Snowy_plains \"Snowy plains\")\n, and [desert](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Desert \"Desert\")\n biomes. In [_Bedrock Edition_](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Bedrock_Edition \"Bedrock Edition\")\n, they also generate in [snowy taiga](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Snowy_taiga \"Snowy taiga\")\n, [sunflower plains](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Sunflower_plains \"Sunflower plains\")\n and any vanilla or custom biome through the [behavior pack](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Behavior_pack \"Behavior pack\")\n that has the `minecraft:village_type` component, which defines the village type and allows it to be generated in that biome. In [_Java Edition_](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Java_Edition \"Java Edition\")\n, villages can generate in [superflat](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Superflat \"Superflat\")\n worlds. The type of the village, and therefore the style of all structures within it, is determined by the biome at the village center or meeting point. The buildings and wood depend on the biome the village is in. If the meeting point does not generate in one of the above biomes, the village defaults to plains style. Village buildings can also be suddenly on the top of a mountain while the other buildings are at the bottom of the mountain. villages\n\nNot to be confused with [Zombie Town](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Zombie_Town \"Zombie Town\")\n.\n\n[](https://minecraft.wiki/w/File:Abandoned_Village.png)\n\nOverview of an abandoned plains village\n\nA village has a 2% chance of generating as an **abandoned village** (also known as **zombie village**).\n\nIn an abandoned village, all generated villagers are instead [zombie villagers](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Zombie_villager \"Zombie villager\")\n, and all [doors](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Door \"Door\")\n and [torches](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Torch \"Torch\")\n are missing. The zombie villagers do not despawn, but have no resistance to sunlight. In abandoned villages, most [cobblestone](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Cobblestone \"Cobblestone\")\n blocks are replaced by [mossy cobblestone](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Mossy_cobblestone \"Mossy cobblestone\")\n, random [blocks](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Block \"Block\")\n (particularly wood) are replaced by [cobwebs](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Cobweb \"Cobweb\")\n, and all glass panes are replaced by [brown stained glass panes](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Brown_Stained_Glass_Pane \"Brown Stained Glass Pane\")\n to represent dirty glass. Abandoned villages also spawn stray [cats](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Cat \"Cat\")\n, as well as the usual village livestock, but they do _not_ spawn [iron golems](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Iron_golem \"Iron golem\")\n naturally. The number of buildings in an abandoned village can be slightly more than in a normal village.\n\n### Location per seed\n\nA preview of village generation is shown below:\n\n1. Apart from the meeting point, which is unique and systematic, the number of buildings of each type is randomly generated and increased in [superflat](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Superflat \"Superflat\")\n‌\\[_[Java Edition](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Java_Edition \"Java Edition\")\\\n only_\\] worlds. More than one meeting point can be generated in superflat worlds. The number of lamp posts and decorative structures (hay bales, melon patch, pumpkin patch, farms, snow and ice patches) has no restriction, as they are generated where no other buildings can be placed. These structures could have functions and could be of great use to the [player](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Player \"Player\")\n. Paths are found between the buildings of the village and often extend beyond them.\n\nStructures are chosen randomly from a pool of possible buildings.\n\nThe full list of the village house blueprints can be accessed by going [here](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Village/Structure/Blueprints \"Village/Structure/Blueprints\")\n.\n\nArchitectural style and blocks making up the village structures vary according to village type. Not every building can be generated in a single village, although some blocks can be found in any village, such as job site blocks and food items.\n\nIn [_Java Edition_](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Java_Edition \"Java Edition\")\n, buildings have different probabilities of generating, depending on village type; for example, a weaponsmith shop is more likely to appear in a Taiga village than in other villages.\n\nIn [_Bedrock Edition_](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Bedrock_Edition \"Bedrock Edition\")\n, villages don't generate with expected structures; for example, a fletcher house doesn't appear in a plains village, and a mason house doesn't appear in a savanna village.\n\n### Paths\n\nVillages generate [paths](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Path \"Path\")\n between the buildings and extend outside of the village. Village paths generate at the level of existing terrain, potentially going up steep hills or down ravines without regard for whether an entity could actually traverse the path. Paths do not go below sea level and replace only [grass blocks](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Grass_block \"Grass block\")\n (with [air](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Air \"Air\")\n above), [water](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Water \"Water\")\n, [lava](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Lava \"Lava\")\n, [sand](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Sand \"Sand\")\n, [sandstone](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Sandstone \"Sandstone\")\n, and [red sandstone](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Red_sandstone \"Red sandstone\")\n; all other blocks are ignored and the blocks underneath are considered for replacement instead.\\[_[verify](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Special:TalkPage/Village \"Special:TalkPage/Village\")\\\n_\\] Villagers use these paths to travel across the village.\n\nIn plains, savanna, taiga, and snowy villages, paths are comprised of [dirt paths](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Dirt_path \"Dirt path\")\n and grass. Savanna villages also generate farmland and crops in some areas. Dirt paths that generate over water are replaced by the village style's plank type. Desert villages are generated with [smooth sandstone](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Smooth_sandstone \"Smooth sandstone\")\n paths.\n\nTrees, lamp posts, and other decorative structures can generate in the middle of paths as obstructions.\n\nLoot\n----\n\nA village loot changes depending on the building.\\[show\\]\\[[edit](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Special:EditPage/Village/Loot \"Special:EditPage/Village/Loot\")\\\n\\]\n\nMain article: [Village/Loot](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Village/Loot \"Village/Loot\")\n\nMechanics\n---------\n\nMain article: [Village mechanics](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Village_mechanics \"Village mechanics\")\n\n### _Java Edition_ villages\n\nVillages as a whole have no defined \"center\", \"size\", or \"radius\"; they are defined only based on proximity to any \"village center\" subchunk.\n\nA [subchunk](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Subchunk \"Subchunk\")\n is a \"village center\" if it contains at least one claimed [bed](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Bed \"Bed\")\n, [bell](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Bell \"Bell\")\n, or [job site](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Job_site \"Job site\")\n block. The 26 subchunks in a 3×3×3 cube around such a subchunk are also considered part of a village.\n\n### _Bedrock Edition_ villages\n\nA village always consists of at least one acceptable bed and one villager. Rarely, a village structure can generate without beds, thus not qualifying as a village. Upon creation, a village center is defined as a POI claimed by the first villager, and the village's size is the greater of 32 blocks or the distance to the furthest bed from the center. Any villager, village golem, or raid-spawned illagers can pathfind back into the village if they find themselves farther than that many blocks from the center.\n\nVillages are established by the number of valid [beds](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Bed \"Bed\")\n in the village.\n\nThe maximum population of a village is the number of valid beds. If the population drops below that point (due to death or removal), but there are at least two villagers left who can reach each other, the villagers mate and [breed](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Villager#Breeding \"Villager\")\n until the population is at the maximum.\n\nA village is created when at least one villager links to one bed. The village continues to exist as long as one of its villagers remains linked to one of its beds. If all beds are unlinked (by being destroyed, by players sleeping in them, or by villagers failing to pathfind to them), then the village ceases to exist. When this happens, the villagers lose all links to job site blocks and bells and cannot use them.\n\nWhen the first villager links to a bed, a village of size 65×25×65 blocks is created, centered on the pillow of that bed. The boundaries, and consequently the center (which is important because it defines where cats and iron golems can spawn), may change as other villagers link or unlink from point of interest (POI) blocks. When the boundaries change, the center usually shifts to the location of the POI block near the midpoint between the farthest out POI in each direction. In naturally generated villages, there is usually a bell near the village center, but aside from that, bells have no special role distinct from other POI in how the game defines and manages the village center and boundaries.\n\n### Gathering site\n\nVillages have gathering sites where villagers may mingle. A gathering site is defined as a [bell](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Bell \"Bell\")\n located within the village boundary. A [wandering trader](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Wandering_trader \"Wandering trader\")\n may spawn at a gathering site, accompanied by trader [llamas](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Llama \"Llama\")\n. A villager also rings the bell when a raid starts.\n\n### Job site blocks\n\n[Job site](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Job_site \"Job site\")\n blocks are blocks such as [grindstones](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Grindstone \"Grindstone\")\n, [smithing tables](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Smithing_table \"Smithing table\")\n, and [lecterns](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Lectern \"Lectern\")\n, which are used by villagers. Villagers with the corresponding professions spend their time in front of their job site block, except for nitwits, baby villagers, and unemployed villagers (villagers without profession overlays). Upon claiming a job site block, green particles appear above both the villager and the job site block, and the villager takes up the profession of the job site block if unemployed. Villagers who have already been traded with can claim only job site blocks related to their profession. Employed villagers who are not linked to a job site block are unable to restock their trades. Villagers cannot link to a job site block that has already been claimed by another villager. There are thirteen job site blocks in the game, each linking to its respective villager profession.\n\nEvents\n------\n\nThese events are not tied to generated village structures, but these structures (except for abandoned villages) typically satisfy the game's definition in the context of [village mechanics](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Village_mechanics \"Village mechanics\")\n. Specifically, these events consider any [chunk](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Chunk \"Chunk\")\n section (aka. \"[subchunk](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Subchunk \"Subchunk\")\n\") within a 3×3×3 cube of sections centered on a section containing a [bed](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Bed \"Bed\")\n, [bell](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Bell \"Bell\")\n, or [job site](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Job_site \"Job site\")\n as part of a village.\n\n### Raids\n\nMain article: [Raid](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Raid \"Raid\")\n\nA player who drinks an [ominous bottle](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Ominous_bottle \"Ominous bottle\")\n (dropped by [raid captains](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Raid_captain \"Raid captain\")\n) receives the [Bad Omen](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Bad_Omen \"Bad Omen\")\n effect for 100 minutes. Like other status effects, Bad Omen can also be cleared by dying or drinking milk. Entering a village boundary while the effect is active turns it into [Raid Omen](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Raid_Omen \"Raid Omen\")\n, which starts a raid after the effect runs out. The [raid](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Raid \"Raid\")\n spawns groups of [illagers](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Illager \"Illager\")\n in waves, which attack the village. The higher the level, the higher the chance for the raiding mobs to wield enchanted weapons.\n\n### Zombie sieges\n\nMain article: [Zombie siege](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Zombie_siege \"Zombie siege\")\n\n[](https://minecraft.wiki/w/File:Information_icon.svg \"File:Information icon.svg\")\n\nThis feature is exclusive to [_Java Edition_](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Java_Edition \"Java Edition\")\n.\n\n \n\nZombie sieges are in-game events where many zombies spawn in a village, regardless of how well-lit or walled off a village is. They have a 10% chance of occurring at midnight every night or during thunderstorms when a village has at least 20 valid beds. There is no indication of a zombie siege happening except for an unusually high number of zombies.\n\nData values\n-----------\n\n### ID\n\n_[Java Edition](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Java_Edition \"Java But they found that it was computationally expensive to evaluate what constituted a house, so to make it simple, they decided that a door with an inside and outside counts as a house; however, the house was later changed to beds.\n*   Because [feature](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Feature \"Feature\")\n     placement is done after structure, clay [disks](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Disks \"Disks\")\n     may generate under water in village farms, even in biomes where clay is rare.\n*   Occasionally, surface [canyons](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Canyons \"Canyons\")\n     and noise [caves](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Caves \"Caves\")\n     generate through villages, causing missing pathways or even entire buildings sunken into the ravine.\n*   In rare cases, players can find a tiny village consisting of one house, or even just one job site-building (a building with job site blocks) without any houses (building with beds), causing the village to not spawn any villagers.\n\nGallery\n-------\n\n### Screenshots\n\n*   [](https://minecraft.wiki/w/File:IglooVillage.png \"An igloo structure that generated in a snowy plains village\")\n    \n    An

Generation\")\\\n | [edit source](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Stronghold?action=edit&section=1 \"Edit section's source code: Generation\")\\\n\\]\n\nFor locating strongholds, see [Eye of Ender § Locating strongholds](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Eye_of_Ender#Locating_strongholds \"Eye of Ender\")\n.\n\n[](https://minecraft.wiki/w/File:EndPortalRoom.png)\n\nThe [End portal](https://minecraft.wiki/w/End_portal \"End portal\")\n room\n\nStrongholds generate at any Y level, mostly underground, and prefer to generate in [biomes](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Biome \"Biome\")\n above sea level, but generate underwater if necessary. Strongholds may generate at [bedrock](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Bedrock \"Bedrock\")\n level, cutting the bedrock.\n\nMost parts of the stronghold can be overwritten by [caves](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Cave \"Cave\")\n, [ocean monuments](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Ocean_monument \"Ocean monument\")\n, [ancient cities](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Ancient_City \"Ancient City\")\n, [mineshafts](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Mineshaft \"Mineshaft\")\n, [fossils](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Fossil \"Fossil\")\n, [amethyst geodes](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Amethyst_geode \"Amethyst geode\")\n, [trial chambers](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Trial_chambers \"Trial chambers\")\n, and [monster rooms](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Monster_room \"Monster room\")\n; but it is rare for the [End portal](https://minecraft.wiki/w/End_portal \"End portal\")\n to be replaced, which would force a player to find one of the other strongholds, as a portal can be activated only with all 12 [End portal frames](https://minecraft.wiki/w/End_portal_frame \"End portal frame\")\n present. [Canyons](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Canyon \"Canyon\")\n do not overwrite strongholds; rather, a stronghold can occasionally be found exposed by a canyon.\n\n### _Bedrock Edition_\n\n\\[[edit](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Stronghold?section=2&veaction=edit \"Edit section: Bedrock Edition\")\\\n | [edit source](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Stronghold?action=edit&section=2 \"Edit section's source code: Bedrock Edition\")\\\n\\]\n\nIn [_Bedrock Edition_](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Bedrock_Edition \"Bedrock Edition\")\n, strongholds generate randomly throughout the world and they are at least 160 blocks away from the world origin. There is no limit to the number of strongholds that can generate, unlike in _Java Edition_. There are 3 extra strongholds at least 453 blocks away from the world origin, which tend to be close to the 453-block boundary, and are guaranteed to generate below meeting points of a [village](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Village \"Village\")\n. In rare cases, these 3 extra strongholds can be very far from the world origin, sometimes over 10,000 blocks away.\n\n### _Java Edition_\n\n\\[[edit](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Stronghold?section=3&veaction=edit \"Edit section: Java Edition\")\\\n | [edit source](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Stronghold?action=edit&section=3 \"Edit section's source code: Java Edition\")\\\n\\]\n\n[](https://minecraft.wiki/w/File:Strongholds_1.9.svg)\n\nStronghold layout through the world in [_Java Edition_](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Java_Edition \"Java Edition\")\n\nIn [_Java Edition_](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Java_Edition \"Java Edition\")\n, all strongholds are located at random coordinates within rings in all biomes, where each ring is a certain radius from the center of the world (X=0, Z=0). For a given ring, the strongholds are generated at roughly equal angles from the center point of the world (for instance, each stronghold in a ring of 3 is in the region of 120 degrees from the others, measured from the origin). The game does not generate a stronghold partially above ground; any portion above the sea level is replaced with [air](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Air \"Air\")\n, leaving a cutaway. If a stronghold generates in an [ocean](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Ocean \"Ocean\")\n, which occurs rarely, it typically generates with terrain covered above it. Strongholds are biased towards biomes in the `[#stronghold_biased_to](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Biome_tag_(Java_Edition)#stronghold_biased_to \"Biome tag (Java Edition)\") ` biome tag.​\\[_[more information needed](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Special:TalkPage/Stronghold \"Special:TalkPage/Stronghold\")\\\n_\\]\n\nThere are eight rings, containing 128 strongholds in total:\n\n1.  The first ring has 3 strongholds within 1,280–2,816 blocks of the origin.\n2.  The second ring has 6 strongholds within 4,352–5,888 blocks of the origin.\n3.  The third ring has 10 strongholds within 7,424–8,960 blocks of the origin.\n4.  The fourth ring has 15 strongholds within 10,496–12,032 blocks of the origin.\n5.  The fifth ring has 21 strongholds within 13,568–15,104 blocks of the origin.\n6.  The sixth ring has 28 strongholds within 16,640–18,176 blocks of the origin.\n7.  The seventh ring has 36 strongholds within 19,712–21,248 blocks of the origin.\n8.  The eighth and outermost ring has 9 strongholds within 22,784–24,320 blocks of the origin.\n\nThe number of strongholds in each ring is the [triangular number](https://oeis.org/A000217)\n of the number one more than the ring number, except for the outermost ring, where 45 would be expected.\n\nThe outermost ring has the further unusual property of putting the strongholds at angles of roughly 36° (not the expected 40°) as if there were to be ten equally spaced strongholds, like in the third ring. One of these ten does not generate, however, keeping the number at nine.\n\nStructure\n---------\n\n\\[[edit](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Stronghold?section=4&veaction=edit \"Edit section: Structure\")\\\n | [edit source](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Stronghold?action=edit&section=4 \"Edit section's source code: Structure\")\\\n\\]\n\nMain article: [/Structure](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Stronghold/Structure \"Stronghold/Structure\")\n\n[](https://minecraft.wiki/w/File:Stronghold_isometric_view.png)\n\nAn isometric view of a stronghold in a [superflat](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Superflat \"Superflat\")\n world.[\\[n 1\\]](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Stronghold#cite_note-1)\n\nStronghold generation starts with a spiral staircase room that leads down to a five-way intersection.

# Slime

Slimes spawn in two places: swamp biomes at night when the moon is bright enough, and deep underground in specific chunks that were designated slime chunks at world generation.

The chunk designation is invisible and permanent and has nothing to do with what the chunk looks like. A cave that produces Slimes will always produce Slimes. A cave that doesn't, won't. Finding a slime chunk underground requires either luck — mining and encountering them — or external tools that calculate chunk seed values. Once found, a slime chunk at the right depth is a reliable and indefinite Slimeball source.

They split. A large Slime hit enough times breaks into medium Slimes. Medium Slimes break into small Slimes. Small Slimes drop Slimeballs and die. The fight against a large Slime is a fight against a progressively multiplying problem — the moment it splits, you're managing more targets, each smaller and faster than the original. Small Slimes are the most annoying because they're quick and low to the ground and difficult to hit cleanly.

Slimeballs are the product. Slimeballs make Slime blocks, which have unusual physics — entities bounce on them, they dampen fall damage, they stick to adjacent blocks when moved by pistons but don't move those blocks along with them. Slime blocks are one of the fundamental components of flying machines and complex redstone contraptions. The Slime is the origin point of a significant portion of engineering in this world.

Magma Cubes are the Nether equivalent. Same splitting behavior, fire immune, drop Magma Cream instead of Slimeballs, slightly more dangerous because of where they live.

They're not hostile in the way that most hostile mobs are hostile. Small Slimes can't hurt you at all — their damage is effectively zero. They hop toward you anyway with an enthusiasm that seems disconnected from any threat they represent. That cheerful purposeless aggression is either endearing or unsettling and most people land somewhere in the middle.

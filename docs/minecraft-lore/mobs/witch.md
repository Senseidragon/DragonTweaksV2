# Witch

The story about Witches and lightning is true, mostly.

A Villager struck by lightning becomes a Witch. The transformation is immediate — the robes, the hat, the permanent stoop, the habit of throwing splash potions at anything that comes close. Whether this is a curse, a conversion, or just what lightning does to people in this world is a question the world doesn't answer directly. What it implies is that the Witch was a person once and that something in the air or the sky or the particulars of this place changed that.

The hut in the swamp came first or the Witch chose it — the sequence is unclear. Small structure on stilts, a cauldron, a black cat inside, a Witch inside with the cat. The cat doesn't seem to belong to the Witch in any meaningful sense. It simply cohabits the space with the same indifference cats apply to most situations. The Witch tolerates it. Maybe the Witch likes cats. There are worse arrangements for someone living alone in a swamp.

She fights with potions. Splash potions of Harming, Slowness, Poison, Weakness — thrown with surprising accuracy at whatever she's decided is a threat. She also uses potions defensively: Fire Resistance when she catches fire, Healing when she's injured, Swiftness when fleeing. The behavior is sophisticated for what the world otherwise categorizes as a hostile mob. She's not attacking blindly. She's managing the situation with available tools, which is something most threats in this world don't bother doing.

The resistance to conventional attack that the potions provide makes her disproportionately difficult relative to her apparent frailty. She'll poison you and then throw Slowness while you're waiting for the poison to run out. She'll heal while you're trying to outlast her.

She drops potion ingredients. Glowstone dust, redstone, spider eyes, sticks, glass bottles. The components of the craft she practices. Killing the Witch for her materials is a grim kind of trade, and maybe the most honest summary of most relationships this world permits.

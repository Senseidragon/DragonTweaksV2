# Warden

It doesn't have eyes.

That's the first thing you notice, or rather the first thing you notice is that you're looking for eyes and there aren't any. Just that ribcage silhouette, the bioluminescent soul light pulsing in its chest, the antennae sweeping the dark. It's listening for you. It's been listening for longer than you've been down here.

The deep dark produces the Warden the way deep water produces pressure — not as a creature that lives there but as a property of the place itself. It emerges from sculk when the sensors decide something has been too loud for too long. Emergence is the right word. It doesn't spawn so much as it rises, shaking off the ground like the earth decided to stand up.

Don't fight it. That's the plainest advice and the most ignored. The Warden has more health than anything you're likely to encounter before the end and hits hard enough to kill in two strikes through good armor. It's not a combat encounter — it's a pressure system you have to navigate around. Every sound you make adds to its anger. Wool muffles your footsteps. Snowball thrown to the far side of the room gives you four seconds. Projectiles shot at sensors give you less.

If it locks onto you anyway, run. Not deeper into the ancient city — up. It doesn't follow far and it can't fit through small gaps.

The soul light in its chest is worth looking at if you're in a position to observe safely. It pulses in a slow rhythm, something between breathing and heartbeat, and the glow it casts is the same blue as the sculk and the same blue as souls and the same blue as the deep dark's particular variety of darkness. Everything down here is the same color. Everything down here is the same thing.

It roars when it locks onto a target. The roar has a physical component — nearby armor takes durability damage, nearby players take a debuff. The sound alone is worth respecting as a signal, if not for the damage, then for what it tells you about what you've done wrong.

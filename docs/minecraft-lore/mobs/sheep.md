# Sheep

Wool comes in sixteen colors and Sheep come pre-dyed in most of them, which means the landscape is occasionally punctuated by a pink Sheep or a blue Sheep standing in a field as if this is normal, which in this world it is.

The standard colors — white, light gray, gray, black, brown — are common. Pink is rare. The others require dye. You can dye a Sheep and then shear it and then wait for regrowth and shear it again, which converts a dyeing operation into an infinite supply of that color wool. The Sheep is a renewable fiber source that accepts color input and produces color output indefinitely, which makes it one of the more elegant resource systems in the game once you understand it.

Shearing is better than killing. A Sheep killed for wool drops one or two blocks. A Sheep sheared drops two to four and survives to grow more. The wool grows back when the Sheep eats grass, which it does on its own without requiring attention. A sheared Sheep in a field with grass will have wool again within a few minutes. The infrastructure almost manages itself.

Mutton is the other product. Less food value than beef, more common than pork in early areas where Sheep outnumber Pigs. It's adequate. It's not the reason you maintain a sheep pen but it's a reasonable side benefit of the operation.

The Evoker turns blue Sheep red. This has been true since Evokers were introduced and no one has fully explained why the Evoker specifically targets blue Sheep or what the significance of the transformation to red is meant to be. It happens rarely because Evokers are found deep in Woodland Mansions, far from any Sheep. The conditions for observing it require either unusual circumstances or deliberate engineering. It remains one of those behaviors that sits in the world unexplained, which is its own kind of notable.

Babies are small and do not produce wool. They grow. The waiting is the only part of sheep husbandry that requires patience.

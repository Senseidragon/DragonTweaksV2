# Bee

Bees live in hives in oak and birch trees in plains, sunflower plains, flower forests, and meadows. The nest is a small block tucked into the branches, and the Bees exit in the morning and return at night, carrying pollen between flowers and their home in a loop that produces honey over time.

They're neutral. Hurt one, or destroy the hive without smoking it first, and every Bee in the vicinity becomes hostile. The sting poisons. The stinger remains in the Bee — unlike actual bees, which leave the stinger and die — and they can sting repeatedly, which means a cluster of agitated Bees is a sustained poison problem rather than a one-and-done encounter. The hostility persists for a while and spreads to nearby Bees who witness the attack.

A campfire placed beneath a hive before harvesting it is the protocol. The smoke calms them. Bottle the honey from a full hive without incident. The campfire-and-bottle method is one of those interactions that requires knowing the trick before it works, and the trick, once known, makes the whole operation quiet and productive.

Honey blocks have unusual physics — they slow movement through them and prevent fall damage if you fall against a honey-covered surface. They're sticky in redstone contexts. The Bee produces a block with three distinct mechanical properties, which is more than most mobs contribute to the material game.

Bee nests attached to trees produce no honey unless flowers are nearby and the Bees are actively working. The Bees need something to do. An isolated tree with a nest in a barren landscape has Bees that exist but accomplish nothing. Move them — lure them with flowers — to a location where they can work and the system activates. It's one of the few cases in this world where a neutral mob's productivity depends on the quality of its environment rather than just its presence.

Honeycomb from shearing is used for wax, for candles, for decorative blocks. The Bee is small but it touches a surprising number of crafting trees.

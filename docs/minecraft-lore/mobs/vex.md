# Vex

The Vex is summoned. It doesn't exist in the world until an Evoker calls it into being, three at a time, small and winged and immediately hostile.

They're roughly the size of a baby mob and they fly, which means they don't interact with terrain the way grounded enemies do. They fly through walls. Through floors. Through any solid block they choose to pass through. The geometry of a room that you've been using to manage spacing and line of sight becomes irrelevant when the threats are operating on a different set of physical rules than you are.

The attack is a sword strike. Small, fast, deals more damage than something that size has any business dealing. In groups of three while you're also managing an Evoker and that Evoker is generating spectral fangs from the floor beneath you, three Vexes are enough to change the outcome of a fight. The priority in any Evoker encounter is to deal with the Vexes quickly, because they will not stop and they do not tire and the Evoker will summon more.

They glow faintly blue. In the dark of a Woodland Mansion room, before you've fully registered that an Evoker is present, the glow of incoming Vexes is often the first signal. By then they're already close.

They have a lifespan. If the Evoker that summoned them is killed, the Vexes persist but they begin taking damage over time. They're not permanent — the summoning has a duration built into it and they deteriorate without the Evoker's presence sustaining them. Kill the Evoker, survive the remaining Vexes, and the problem solves itself given enough time.

They drop iron swords sometimes. A Vex, summoned from nowhere by a robed figure in a haunted mansion, carrying a sword it could barely swing given its size, that deals damage disproportionate to its small frame. The world contains many things that don't entirely make sense if you examine them closely. The Vex is one of them.

# Horse

Horses are faster than walking and that simple fact shapes how you think about distance.

They spawn in plains and savannas in small herds, neutral, wandering. To tame one you mount it — no item required, just right-click — and it bucks you off. You try again. It bucks you off again. The taming is a patience mechanic: each attempt raises a hidden temper value, and when that value crosses the threshold, the horse accepts you. Hearts appear and the horse stops bucking. Feed it apples, wheat, or sugar between attempts to raise the temper faster.

A tamed horse needs a saddle before it can be steered. Saddles come from chests in dungeons, temples, villages, bastions — they're not craftable. This creates a dependency: you have the horse, you need the saddle, which means you need to explore. The horse is both the reward for early exploration and the motivation for more of it.

Speed, health, and jump height are all random within ranges and are set at spawn. A fast horse with good jump height is worth keeping and breeding. Horses breed with golden apples or golden carrots, and the foal's stats are derived from its parents — averages with some random variance. Selective breeding over multiple generations can produce horses noticeably better than anything you'd find naturally.

Armor fits Horses but not other rideable mobs. Iron, gold, diamond, leather horse armor with varying protection values. Diamond horse armor on a high-health horse in the late game represents a significant investment that keeps a valuable animal alive in dangerous territory.

Donkeys and Mules carry chests. Horses don't. The tradeoff is speed versus storage — a Horse moves faster and takes armor, a Donkey or Mule brings inventory. For long-range travel the Horse wins. For moving materials across distances, the chest-bearing alternative has its own argument.

The sound of hooves on different surfaces is one of the better ambient details in the world. Plains travel on horseback is a different experience than on foot, and the difference is worth finding.

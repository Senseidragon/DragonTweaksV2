# Pillager

The Pillager is the rank and file of the Illager operation. They staff the outposts, they participate in raids, they patrol in patrols that roam between outposts and villages with a purposefulness that suggests organization rather than wandering.

They carry crossbows. The crossbow distinguishes them from Skeletons — slower rate of fire, heavier bolt, can be pre-loaded before closing distance. A Pillager with a loaded crossbow will fire as soon as they have line of sight. They reload in the field. They can sprint while loading, which changes the approach calculus.

The outpost captain carries a Banner of Omen. Kill the captain and you acquire the Bad Omen effect, which triggers a raid if you then walk into a village. This is either a hazard or a means of initiating a raid on purpose, depending on what you want. Raids produce Emeralds, Hero of the Village status, and discounts on Villager trades. The mechanics have been thought through. There's a game here beyond just surviving the initial aggression.

Patrols are the overworld encounter. A small group, four or five, walking in rough formation across the landscape. They'll attack on sight, which means the moment you crest a hill and see them you're already in their detection range. The captain is identifiable by the banner. Kill the patrol and you have a choice about whether to absorb the Bad Omen.

What makes Pillagers interesting as enemies isn't their individual difficulty — they're manageable one on one — it's the infrastructure they represent. Outposts, patrols, raid mechanics, the Banner of Omen economy. They're not just a mob that attacks you. They're part of a faction with a presence in the world, a relationship to villages, a coherent if hostile social structure. They're enemies in the way that an opposing force with logistics and objectives is an enemy, rather than in the way that a random night spawn is an enemy.

That distinction matters when you're deciding how to approach them.

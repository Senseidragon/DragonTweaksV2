# Skeleton

The Skeleton is the first enemy that will kill you at range, and it will do so reliably and without drama.

They appear at night and in dark spaces: underground, in dense forest, anywhere the light level drops below seven. They carry a bow and they use it immediately. The shot leads the target — if you're strafing they'll adjust. If you're standing still you'll get hit. The correct response is to close distance fast, because Skeletons are significantly worse at melee than at range, and because a Skeleton at close quarters is mostly trying to back away rather than fight.

They're fragile. Less health than a Zombie, lighter armor, no real ability to respond to sustained close pressure. The challenge is closing the gap when they start shooting from forty blocks away and hitting accurately while you're sprinting across open ground.

Sunlight kills them, which gives the whole problem a convenient daily resolution. Any Skeleton caught outside at dawn catches fire and burns. This creates a pattern of hearing the crackling of burning bones right after sunrise, which becomes unremarkable after enough mornings. You stop noticing it the way you stop noticing other regular sounds — it's just the world resetting.

They sometimes wear armor. Helmets protect from sun damage, meaning an armored Skeleton survived the sunrise and is still standing at noon in the middle of a field. Enchanted bows are possible drops, which is the game asking you to consider whether the risk of getting shot is worth whatever the bow might provide.

Strays are the cold-weather variant — same Skeleton, different coat, arrows tipped with Slowness. The Slowness is specifically punishing given that closing distance is already the challenge. A Stray in an open snowy field is genuinely more dangerous than its temperate counterpart for exactly this reason.

The bones and arrows they drop are useful enough that Skeletons become a resource at some point rather than just a threat. You start thinking about where to find them, how to position yourself to collect efficiently. The enemy becomes a material. That transition happens quietly and is one of many small ways this world changes how you think about things.

# Polar Bear

Polar Bears are neutral until they're not, and the trigger is specific.

They spawn in snowy biomes — snowy plains, frozen peaks, ice spikes, snowy taiga — usually alone or as a parent-cub pair. A lone adult is neutral: it ignores you, you ignore it, everyone continues. Get between a parent and its cub and the adult attacks, and the attack is not a minor inconvenience. Polar Bears hit hard and take damage well and don't stop until you leave the area or one of you is dead.

The cub doesn't attack. The cub does what cubs do, which is exist near the adult and be the reason the adult is dangerous. Keep your distance from pairs. The rule is simple and occasionally hard to follow in terrain where visibility is limited and you've stumbled into a family before registering what you're looking at.

They move across snow and ice with a weight and purpose that other mobs in the snowy biome don't have. Rabbits are quick and skittish. Strays are distant threats. The Polar Bear occupies the snowy plains differently — large, calm, present. It belongs there in a way that reads clearly even if you can't articulate why.

They drop raw cod or raw salmon on death. Occasionally a fishing rod. The fishing rod drop from a Polar Bear is one of those item drops that doesn't quite make sense if you think about it and doesn't matter if you don't.

There's no taming. There's no breeding. There's no trade or interaction beyond the neutral/hostile toggle. The Polar Bear is there, it's large, and it has a condition under which it becomes a problem. Most encounters involve seeing it and giving it appropriate space, which is probably the correct relationship to have with a Polar Bear in any context.

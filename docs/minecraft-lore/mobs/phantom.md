# Phantom

Don't sleep.

That's the advice, and it's not metaphorical. If you go three days without sleeping in a bed, Phantoms start spawning at night. They circle overhead, diving periodically, making a sound like tearing canvas. They're not interested in anything except you. They came because you didn't sleep and they will keep coming every night until you do.

The game is enforcing something with the Phantom. It has a mechanic that tracks how long you've been awake and builds a consequence proportional to your neglect. Three days is the threshold. After that, the sky becomes hostile in a specific and personal way. The Phantoms are yours — they spawned for you, they target you, and they will follow you through the night like a debt that won't be discharged until you rest.

They burn in sunlight, which gives the nights a natural endpoint. Survive until dawn and they dissolve. But they spawn again the next night if you still haven't slept, and in larger numbers. The sky gets worse the longer you ignore it.

Membrane is what they drop. Phantom Membrane, used in the brewing of potions of Slow Falling, used in repairing Elytra. They have an economy: the inconvenience of dealing with them produces material used to not fall to your death. The world gives you a reason to be annoyed by them and a reason to collect their remains anyway.

Cats are relevant here too. Phantoms, like Creepers, avoid cats. There's something comforting about this if you're building a base that needs to be defended against the consequences of poor sleep habits. A cat on the roof addresses two different threats.

Sleep. That's still the simplest solution. Lay down, skip the night, reset the counter. The Phantoms won't appear. The sky will stay empty. Everything the Phantom represents — the consequence of forgetting to rest, the way the world eventually charges you for the debt — goes away as soon as you close your eyes.

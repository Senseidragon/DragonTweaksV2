# Illusioner

The Illusioner doesn't appear in normal play.

It exists in the game's files. It has behaviors, attacks, a full implementation. It can be summoned with commands. It just doesn't spawn naturally — not in Mansions, not in raids, not in any biome or structure. It occupies the same category as a few other things in the world: fully realized, never encountered unless you specifically go looking.

What it does, when you find it: it casts Blindness on you, which reduces your vision to a small circle of normal sight surrounded by dark. While you're blinded it creates four illusory copies of itself and starts shooting. The copies move. The copies shoot. You cannot easily tell which one is real. You shoot at silhouettes through impaired vision and try to determine which one actually takes damage.

The mechanic is clever. Blindness as a combat opener, illusory duplicates to split attention and make targeting difficult, sustained ranged attack while you're working through both problems. It would be genuinely difficult in a Woodland Mansion context, where you're already managing Evokers and Vindicators and Vexes in enclosed spaces. The decision not to include it in normal encounters is either restraint or unfinished business, and which of those is true isn't documented anywhere accessible.

Players who summon them report that they're tough — not uniquely so, but tough enough that the Blindness opener in close quarters requires an immediate and effective counter. Moving out of range, using a bucket of milk if available, fighting by sound and memory of where it was standing.

There's something fitting about an Illusioner that most players never see. A mob that creates false versions of itself, encountered only if you summon it yourself, whose existence is technically present and practically absent. The Illusioner is an illusion in a more fundamental sense than it intends to be.

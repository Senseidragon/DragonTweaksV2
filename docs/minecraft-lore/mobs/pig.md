# Pig

The Pig is straightforward in a way that few things in this world are.

It wanders. It follows carrots. It eats, it breathes, it produces porkchop when it dies. There's no secondary system attached to it, no biome variant with special properties, no mechanic that requires understanding before you can interact with it correctly. It is a pig. It does pig things. You either need pork or you don't.

Two exceptions to the straightforwardness. The first: lightning. A Pig struck by lightning becomes a Zombified Piglin. Not a Zombie Pig — something different, something that has one foot in the Nether and one foot in whatever the Pig used to be. The transformation is immediate and total. One moment a placid pink animal, then a flash, then something with a golden sword and a grievance. The world's habit of using lightning as a transformation vector applies to Pigs as readily as to Villagers, which implies something about lightning or about the nature of things in this world that absorb it.

The second: saddled and ridden with a carrot on a stick. A saddled Pig can be mounted and steered by a player holding a carrot on a stick, which functions as both a lure and a throttle. The Pig runs toward the carrot it can never reach while the rider goes wherever they want. The metaphor writes itself and the game doesn't comment on it.

They spawn in groups, which means finding one usually means finding several. A forest with a few Pigs visible from a distance has more Pigs visible once you get closer. Breeding with carrots, potatoes, or beetroot produces piglets that grow into adults. The herd management is the same as any other passive mob — find two, feed them, expand.

Porkchop cooked is among the best food items available. High saturation, stackable, the Pig produces it efficiently given how common Pigs are and how straightforward their breeding is. The Pig is not interesting. The Pig is useful. In a survival context, useful is what you need.

# Elder Guardian

There are three in every Ocean Monument. One in each wing. They don't leave.

The Elder Guardian is the Guardian's larger, slower, more deliberate counterpart, and it has a trick the regular Guardians don't: Mining Fatigue. A status effect it projects through the surrounding water periodically, a ghostly face appearing on your screen, your arm slowing to something barely faster than a standstill. Mining Fatigue III means you cannot break blocks at any practical speed. You cannot dig through a wall to escape. You cannot tunnel your way to the sponge rooms or the treasure chamber. You have to do it the other way — through the corridors, through the Guardians, through whatever the Monument puts in front of you — because the Elder Guardian has made the shortcut inaccessible.

They're territorial in the way fixed bosses are territorial. They patrol slowly. Their laser attack — same as the regular Guardian, a sustained beam that builds damage, that can be interrupted by moving out of line of sight — is slower to charge but hits harder. In the enclosed spaces of the Monument, where every corridor is flooded and visibility is limited, getting out of line of sight requires going around corners you may not have time to find.

Potions of Water Breathing and Night Vision are the two items that make an Ocean Monument run survivable rather than punishing. Without them, you're working against time and dark water in addition to the Guardians. With them, the monument becomes a logistics problem rather than a drowning problem. It's still a hard logistics problem.

Sponges are the treasure, practically speaking. The sponge rooms exist precisely because you need sponges to drain sections of the Monument if you want to do anything with the interior. The Elder Guardians are protecting the tools you'd need to dismantle them. Whether that's elegant or cruel depends on your relationship with the game's sense of humor.

They drop wet sponge and a Sponge block, sometimes a Prismarine Shard, sometimes a fish. The fish feels like an afterthought. The sponge is the point.

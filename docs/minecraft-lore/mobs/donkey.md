# Donkey

The Donkey carries things. That's the whole job and it does it well.

Tame one the same way you tame a Horse — mount it repeatedly until it accepts you, signaled by hearts. Then attach a chest with a right-click while holding the chest. The Donkey now has fifteen inventory slots accessible while you're riding it or by sneaking and interacting. Those fifteen slots move with the Donkey, which moves with you, which means the Donkey is essentially a mobile storage unit with legs and an opinion about its walking speed.

They're slower than Horses. Noticeably slower. The tradeoff is explicit: you can move faster on a Horse, but the Horse can't carry your materials. Long-distance material transport — moving iron from a mining camp to a base, relocating a chest's worth of building blocks — is the Donkey's use case. A Horse gets you there faster. The Donkey brings more with you.

Mules are the Horse-Donkey hybrid, bred from one of each. Mules can't be bred further but they carry chests like Donkeys and move faster than Donkeys. They're the practical middle option when you've already invested in Horse breeding and want the storage without giving up all the speed.

Donkeys spawn in plains and savannas alongside Horses, in the same herds, at lower frequency. They're less dramatically impressive than Horses — no color variants, no armor slot, no speed records — and they do their job without requiring any particular appreciation. A Donkey with a chest attached is one of the most quietly useful things in the early-to-mid game.

Feed them wheat, apples, hay bales to heal. They breed with golden apples or golden carrots the same as Horses. A bred Donkey foal inherits its parents' stats, though Donkey stat variance matters less than Horse stat variance given that you're not optimizing for speed.

# Wolf

Wolves are the oldest alliance in this world.

Find them in forests and taigas, in packs of two to eight, neutral until provoked. Hitting one makes every Wolf in the pack hostile simultaneously — they share aggression, which makes the mistake of hitting one in a pack notably more consequential than it first appears. Avoid this.

Tame one with bones. It takes multiple — there's no fixed number, just a probability each time, and the Wolf sits there through each attempt with an expression of patient assessment until the collar appears and it becomes yours. The collar is red by default. It can be dyed any color. This has no mechanical significance and every significance in terms of which Wolf is yours when you're managing several.

A tamed Wolf will sit on command or follow you. When following, it attacks anything you attack and anything that attacks you. It has enough health to contribute meaningfully in combat and enough speed to keep up with most threats. Multiple tamed Wolves change the combat calculus significantly — a pack of five will tear through most overworld enemies faster than you can manage solo.

They take damage. They show it — a tamed Wolf's tail angle indicates health, dropping lower as the Wolf loses hit points. Feed it meat to heal it. This is easy to forget and important not to — a Wolf fighting alongside you in a dungeon will absorb hits that would otherwise hit you, and it does this without complaint, and it will die doing it if you don't pay attention.

Wolves eat rotten flesh without the hunger penalty players suffer. Wolves bred together produce puppies. A tamed pair will produce a tamed puppy that inherits the collar and grows into a full companion.

They're loyal in a way that most things in this world aren't. That's either an earned observation or a projection onto something that simply has the right AI behaviors. Either way, the result is the same.

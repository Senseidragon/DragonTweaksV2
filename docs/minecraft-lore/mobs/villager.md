# Villager

Villagers are the closest thing this world has to a functioning economy, and the economy is deeply exploitable if you know what you're doing.

They live in villages, they have professions tied to their job site blocks, and they trade. That's the core of it. Find a Librarian, buy enchanted books. Find a Weaponsmith, buy swords. Find a Cleric, buy bottles o' enchanting and eventually access to the Nether wart supply chain. Each profession has a trade table that unlocks deeper offers as you do business with them — they gain experience from trades, level up, offer better items. A maxed-out Librarian with the right enchantment offers is worth more than most structures in the game.

They sleep. They wake. They wander to their job sites during the day, return to beds at night, ring the bell when Pillagers arrive. The bell triggers a scattering behavior that's less about survival and more about chaos — Villagers running in random directions is not a defense. The iron golem is the defense.

Zombie Villagers exist. A Villager caught by a Zombie becomes one, and a Zombie Villager can be cured: weaken it with a Splash Potion of Weakness, feed it a Golden Apple, wait several minutes while it shakes and groans, and it converts back. The cured Villager gives you a significant discount on its trades permanently. This is one of the most economically important mechanics in the game — a cured Villager sells things for fractions of the normal cost. A village where you've intentionally infected and cured multiple Villagers becomes a discount trade hub that funds the rest of your playthrough.

They don't thank you. They don't acknowledge the golem you built or the raids you fought or the zombie sieges you repelled. They wander to their job sites in the morning and ring the bell and trade with you for emeralds with the same expression they use for everything.

That's fine. The trades are the point.

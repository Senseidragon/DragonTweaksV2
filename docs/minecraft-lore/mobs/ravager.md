# Ravager

The Ravager is what a raid looks like when it stops being manageable.

It's large — larger than anything else that walks into a village on raid night — and it moves fast for its size, and it hits like the architectural consequence of everything it's walking through. Fences break. Leaves vanish. Crops get torn up by the simple fact of its passage. It doesn't need to specifically target your infrastructure. It damages it by existing in the same space.

The attack is a lunge and bite. It stuns on impact — a brief rooted delay that interrupts whatever you were doing and leaves you standing still while it lines up the follow-through. The stun is the part that kills people. You think you've timed a dodge and then you're frozen and the second hit lands. Managing the Ravager is about avoiding the stun more than it is about dealing damage, because if you're landing hits you're probably close enough to get stunned.

Shields work. A shield raised at the right moment will stagger the Ravager and push it back, creating distance. The window to raise the shield is tight and the timing is specific but it's reliable once you have it. The Ravager becomes much more survivable when you understand that it can be interrupted rather than just absorbed.

It's ridden, sometimes. Pillager on top, directing. The image of it is one of those details that lands harder in person than described — a thing the size of a small structure carrying an archer on its back, both of them in the middle of your village, both of them functional threats. Deal with the rider first. The Ravager without direction is still dangerous but it's single-threaded.

Evokers and Vindicators come in the same wave. The Ravager is not the only problem. It's the loudest problem in a wave designed to exhaust and overwhelm by presenting several large problems simultaneously. A village that makes it through a late raid wave has been genuinely tested.

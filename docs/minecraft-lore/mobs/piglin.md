# Piglin

Piglins have a civilization. It's not a comfortable one to visit, but it has rules.

They live in the Nether, primarily in Crimson Forests and Bastion Remnants. They're hostile on sight unless you're wearing gold armor — any piece of gold armor makes them neutral, makes them treat you as something affiliated with the thing they value rather than an intruder. The gold armor requirement is specific: remove it and they immediately turn hostile. Wear it and you can walk through their spaces without incident.

Bartering. Drop a gold ingot near a Piglin and it picks it up, examines it with the focus of something that has decided gold is important, and then throws back a random item from a fixed loot table. Gravel, obsidian, fire resistance potions, Ender Pearls, Nether Quartz, crying obsidian, Soul Sand, blackstone. The fire resistance potions are the most valuable — they make the Nether navigable without constantly dying to lava — and getting them requires dropping enough gold to hit the right trade. The Piglin economy is real and it runs on gold.

Zombified Piglins are what Piglins become when they enter the overworld or are struck by lightning. The conversion strips the bartering behavior and leaves only aggression — and a Zombified Piglin, once provoked, calls every other Zombified Piglin in range. Provoking one is provoking all of them. The group aggression of Zombified Piglins in the overworld Nether portal area is a common early Nether disaster.

Piglin Brutes are larger, more aggressive, don't respond to gold armor, and guard Bastion Remnants. They're the Piglin equivalent of elite soldiers — same general shape, significantly more dangerous, not interested in trade.

They hunt Hoglins. The Piglin-Hoglin dynamic is one of the few predator-prey relationships in the game, visible and active if you watch a Crimson Forest long enough.

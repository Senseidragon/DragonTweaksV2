# Fox

Foxes are nocturnal and they don't trust you.

They spawn in taigas and snowy taigas, in small groups, sleeping during the day in a curled posture that looks designed to be left alone. Approach one and it wakes and runs. You can't tame an adult Fox — the distrust is built in and permanent. What you can do is breed two Foxes with sweet berries and the resulting kit will trust you, having never learned otherwise. The kit needs to grow up and it will follow you and defend you and bring you things it finds on the ground with the specific enthusiasm of an animal that was raised to think this is normal.

They steal. A Fox near a chicken coop is a problem. A Fox near crops with berries is a problem. They pick up items off the ground and carry them in their mouths, and while they're holding something they won't attack you for it and won't drop it without reason. They'll eat food items they're carrying, which is worth knowing if you're wondering why your dropped cooked chicken disappeared.

Snow Foxes in the snowy taiga, red Foxes in the regular taiga. The coat is the only difference.

They pounce. The jump onto prey — a high vertical leap followed by a nosedive into the ground — is one of the more animated behaviors in the game. They do this hunting chickens and rabbits. You can watch it from a distance and it reads clearly as a predator doing predator things, which is a level of behavioral coherence most mobs don't reach.

They're immune to sweet berry bush damage, which is how they live in environments where the undergrowth would lacerate anything else. A trusted Fox following you through a taiga will walk through berry bushes without flinching while you bleed. This is occasionally useful and consistently unfair.

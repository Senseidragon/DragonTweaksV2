# Spider

Spiders are the only hostile mob that becomes neutral during the day.

At night they're aggressive — they'll track you and attack on sight, climbing walls and ceilings that would stop other threats. The climbing is the distinguishing feature. A moat stops Zombies. A wall stops most things. A Spider goes up the outside of your wall and appears on your roof as casually as if you'd built them a ramp. The standard defensive perimeter doesn't account for vertical movement and Spiders are the reason to reconsider the standard defensive perimeter.

During the day they change. Not passive, not friendly — neutral. They'll ignore you as long as you ignore them. You can walk past a cluster of Spiders in daylight and they'll sit there moving slightly in that unsettling eight-legged way and not chase you. Attack one and they all turn hostile again, individually and collectively. The daytime neutrality is not safety. It's a temporary non-aggression that ends the moment you do anything to disturb it.

Spider Jockeys are Spiders with Skeletons riding them. The Skeleton provides ranged attack while the Spider provides the climbing and the chase speed. They're rare but they're what happens when two problems combine into something worse than either component.

Cave Spiders are the underground variant — smaller, faster, venomous. They spawn from spawner blocks in mineshafts. The venom is the problem: it drains health over time and there's nothing to do but wait it out and hope you've taken enough milk or have enough health remaining. The smaller size means they fit through one-block gaps that regular Spiders can't. Mining in mineshafts involves regular encounters with Cave Spider spawners, which are worth destroying rather than farming unless you specifically want the string and experience.

String is the drop. String becomes wool, becomes bows, becomes tripwire. The Spider is both a threat and a fiber source, which is a useful combination when you're early in a playthrough and need both to be defended and to be equipped.

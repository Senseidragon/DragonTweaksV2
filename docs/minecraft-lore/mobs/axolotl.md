# Axolotl

Axolotls live in lush caves, in the water beneath the azalea trees, in the deep underground where the light is soft and green and nothing else quite like them exists.

They come in five colors — leucistic (pink), wild (brown), gold, cyan, and blue. The blue variant is rare to the point that most players never see one naturally. They're passive toward players and aggressive toward most aquatic mobs — fish, squid, guardians, drowned. An Axolotl fighting an underwater threat will play dead if it takes too much damage: it flips over, becomes temporarily invulnerable, and regenerates health. This is a real biological behavior translated directly into game mechanics, and it works the same way in both contexts.

Catch one in a bucket. That's the transport method — water bucket, pick up the Axolotl, carry the bucket. They die quickly outside of water, so the bucket is the only practical way to move them. A bucket of Axolotl. Breed them with tropical fish buckets. They breed well and the color of the offspring is inherited from the parents with a small chance of mutation.

Take them to ocean monuments. An Axolotl in the water of a monument will fight Guardians and Elder Guardians, and if it's fighting something you're also fighting, it grants you Regeneration and removes Mining Fatigue temporarily when it makes a kill. The Axolotl as tactical asset in the most difficult underwater encounter in the game is one of those synergies that feels designed and probably was.

They were nearly extinct in the real world when they were added to this one. The blue variant's rarity is a reference to that. The game put something endangered in the lush caves and made it rare and worth protecting and worth finding. Whether that was intentional commentary or incidental design decision doesn't change what it is.

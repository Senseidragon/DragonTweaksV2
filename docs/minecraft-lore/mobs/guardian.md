# Guardian

The Guardian is the Ocean Monument's active defense, and the Monument produces them indefinitely.

They're aquatic — they can survive briefly on land but they're built for underwater combat, moving in short bursts, spinning to face threats. The weapon is a laser: a beam that locks onto you, charges over a few seconds with a rising tone, and then delivers a burst of damage. You can interrupt it by breaking line of sight. Swim around a corner, duck behind a pillar, get something solid between you and the beam, and the charge resets. The Monument is full of walls and corners specifically because the Guardians are in there.

The spikes. A Guardian's body is covered in retractable spines that it extends when approached. Hitting a Guardian in melee while the spines are extended deals reflected damage back to the attacker. The spines retract when the Guardian is charging its laser — that's the window for melee. Attack during the laser charge, retreat before the spines come back, repeat. Managing this rhythm while also avoiding the laser and navigating flooded corridors is the Guardian encounter in summary.

They drop raw cod, prismarine shards, and occasionally prismarine crystals. Prismarine is the primary building material of the Monument and the main reason to farm Guardians rather than simply clear the structure and leave. A Guardian farm — a structure that funnels Guardians from spawning to killing automatically — is one of the more complex farms to build but produces prismarine and experience at rates that justify the construction investment.

They make a sound. A grinding, rhythmic sound while they swim that carries through water and through the walls of the Monument. In the dark corridors before you've placed any light sources, you hear them before you see them, which is usually sufficient warning to stop moving and plan.

The Elder Guardian is larger, slower, and carries the Mining Fatigue debuff. The regular Guardians are the encounter. The Elder Guardians are the statement.

# Rabbit

Rabbits are small and fast and largely irrelevant to survival after the first few days, and there's something pleasant about that.

They appear in deserts, snowy biomes, flower forests, and plains. The desert variant is tan, the snowy variant is white, which means the game has given them some level of adaptive coloring even if the adaptation is cosmetic rather than mechanical. They hop around doing nothing in particular. They flee from players and wolves on approach, which makes them difficult to catch and easy to ignore.

Rabbit meat is a minor food source. Rabbit hide is used for leather bundles — four hides craft into a single leather, which is an inefficient conversion that makes rabbits a last resort for leather rather than a first choice. Rabbit's Foot is a potion ingredient, used in Potions of Leaping, dropped rarely. The whole material profile of the Rabbit is minor: low yield, low value, rarely worth pursuing deliberately.

The Killer Bunny is a different matter. White with red eyes, aggressive on sight, deals significant damage per hit. It doesn't exist in normal gameplay — only summonable via command — but it was once a natural spawn, a hostile rabbit hidden among the passive ones. The name is a reference. The decision to keep it in the code as a summonable mob rather than removing it entirely suggests that whoever made that decision understood the value of the reference.

Baby Rabbits have the proportions that make all baby mobs disproportionately appealing — too-large head, bouncing movement, the specific smallness that makes them seem more like toys than animals. This serves no mechanical purpose. It is an aesthetic choice, and it is correct.

Rabbits don't ask for much from the world and don't give much back. They occupy the middle distance of the landscape, quick shapes crossing the grass, gone before you've decided whether to pursue them. Most days you leave them alone.

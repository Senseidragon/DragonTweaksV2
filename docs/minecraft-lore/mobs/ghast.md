# Ghast

The Ghast is large and pale and it cries.

That's the sound — a high, mournful wail that carries across the Nether's open spaces, that you hear before you see the Ghast itself, that you will associate with the specific exposed feeling of being in the Nether without cover. The Ghast floats slowly. It has a nine-block body that makes it one of the largest mobs in the game. Its eyes are closed until it's about to fire.

Fireballs. It launches explosive fireballs that ignite and crater whatever they hit. Netherrack burns. The crater stays. The Nether landscape near any Ghast's range of activity accumulates craters over time, turning the terrain increasingly irregular. Fighting a Ghast across open Nether wastes means navigating a progressively more difficult landscape.

Reflect the fireball. A Ghast's fireball can be hit back with any weapon or projectile — a sword swing, an arrow, another fireball — and if the reflected fireball hits the Ghast, it deals the same damage to the Ghast that it would have dealt to you. This is the primary kill method at range and it requires timing and practice. The window is specific. A missed deflection puts the fireball somewhere else in the Nether and the problem becomes the crater rather than the Ghast.

They cry when they take damage. The same sound they make floating through the Nether, the same cry, now applied to pain rather than whatever they were expressing before. The ambiguity of what a Ghast is communicating when it cries — hostility, sadness, something that doesn't map to either — is one of the more effective ambient details in the world.

Ghast Tears drop on death. They're used in potions of regeneration and in crafting End Crystals. A Ghast Tear is proof that a large pale crying thing died in the Nether and you were the reason.

# Iron Golem

The Iron Golem is the village's immune system. It patrols. It watches. It throws things.

A naturally spawned Iron Golem belongs to the village — it emerged from the village's collective need for protection and it acts accordingly. It will attack any hostile mob that threatens a Villager. The attack is a two-handed upward swing that launches the target into the air, followed by a downward slam when they land. It deals enough damage to kill most overworld threats in two or three hits. Zombies, Pillagers, Vindicators, anything that constitutes a threat to the population it's protecting — the Golem moves toward it with the unhurried certainty of something that knows it's going to win.

You can build one. Four iron blocks in a T-shape, pumpkin on top. The construction is deliberate and the result is loyal to you — a player-constructed Golem won't be attached to a village the way a naturally spawned one is, but it will defend the area you place it in and attack anything hostile nearby. A ring of Iron Golems around a base perimeter is expensive in iron and effective in practice.

They drop iron ingots and poppies. The poppies are the detail worth sitting with. An Iron Golem will sometimes pick a poppy from the ground and offer it to a Villager child, holding out the flower with a gesture that has no mechanical purpose and communicates something about what the Golem is despite what it's made of. It was built to protect. It gives flowers to children. These two facts coexist without contradiction.

Hitting a Villager will anger the village Golem. Enough negative trades — consistently offering bad deals until your reputation drops low enough — will also anger them. The Golem enforces the village's social contract in both directions.

They're slow. Fast enough to catch most things, but a sprinting player can outrun one. That's relevant if you do something you shouldn't.

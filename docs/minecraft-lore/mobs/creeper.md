# Creeper

The thing about Creepers is that they don't make noise.

Every other threat in this world announces itself. Skeletons rattle. Zombies moan. Spiders click. The Creeper walks up behind you in silence and the first indication you have that it exists is a hiss that means you have about one and a half seconds before it takes everything in a six-block radius with it.

They're green, which doesn't help — they blend into grass, into leaves, into the general foliage of anywhere that isn't a desert. At night they're dark shapes that could be anything. The four stubby legs don't make footstep sounds the way bipedal mobs do. This is either an evolved advantage or a cruel coincidence and the distinction doesn't matter much when you're rebuilding your wall.

They don't actually want to explode. That's an important thing to understand about the Creeper. It walks toward you because it wants to be near you, and when it gets close enough it detonates, and in the moment before it detonates it flinches — a brief flash, the hiss, a split second where it knows something is about to happen. It doesn't survive the explosion any more than you do. There's something sad about that if you think about it, which you shouldn't, because thinking about it while one is approaching you is how you end up as a crater.

Cats frighten them. Actual fright — Creepers will actively flee from cats, which makes ocelots and their domesticated descendants disproportionately valuable as base security. A few cats wandering the perimeter will keep Creepers at range more reliably than a wall.

Charged Creepers happen when lightning strikes one. They're the same thing but worse — larger explosion radius, guaranteed mob head drop if the explosion kills another mob. They're rare enough that most people see one and immediately start making decisions they later regret.

The drops are gunpowder and, sometimes, a music disc. The music discs only drop if a Skeleton arrow kills the Creeper rather than a player. This requires a specific arrangement of enemies working against each other, which the game encourages you to engineer. The fact that the game encodes this possibility suggests something about the nature of this world that probably doesn't bear close examination.

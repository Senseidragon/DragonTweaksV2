# Blaze

Blazes are the reason you go to the Nether Fortress, and they are unpleasant to deal with in the environment where you find them.

They float. They rise, they spin, they launch three fireballs in a burst, they sink, they repeat. The fireballs set fire to whatever they hit, which in a Nether Fortress means fire on netherack, which burns indefinitely. The corridors fill with fire. The ledges fill with fire. You're managing navigation and fire avoidance and incoming fireball patterns simultaneously in a structure that was not designed with your comfort in mind.

Snowballs work. A thrown snowball deals damage to a Blaze — three hit kills at full health — and snowballs are stackable and fast to throw. The logic of a cold projectile hurting a fire mob is the logic of fire and water, which the game applies consistently. Carry snow. The Nether has no natural snow, so bring it.

Blaze Rods are the drop. Blaze Rods become Blaze Powder, which becomes fuel for the brewing stand, which means every potion you brew requires Blazes to have died. Blaze Powder also goes into Eyes of Ender, which means the route to the End runs through the Nether Fortress and through killing enough Blazes to craft enough Eyes. The Blaze is a gate in both the brewing and the End progression trees.

They spawn from spawner blocks inside Fortresses. The spawners can be farmed — build the right structure around a Blaze spawner and you have a reliable, renewable source of Blaze Rods for as long as the world exists. The investment is fighting enough Blazes in the initial construction to secure the area, which is the work.

They're on fire constantly. The spinning, the fire, the floating — the Blaze looks like something that was built rather than born, a mechanism with a specific purpose running in a loop. What the purpose is, beyond killing you and producing rods, the Fortress doesn't say.

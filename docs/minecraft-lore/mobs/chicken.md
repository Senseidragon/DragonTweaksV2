# Chicken

The Chicken is the most passive thing in a world that is frequently trying to kill you, and it falls off ledges constantly.

They wander without apparent destination, changing direction at intervals that don't seem connected to anything. They cluck. They occasionally lay an egg, which drops on the ground and can be thrown and has a small chance of spawning a chick, which is one of the more roundabout reproduction mechanics available. They float downward at a reduced rate when they fall — some built-in flutter that prevents fall damage — which means a Chicken dropped from any height survives, lands, and immediately resumes wandering as if nothing happened.

Feathers and chicken meat are the products. Feathers go into arrows, which go into the bow economy, which means Chickens are indirectly part of the ranged combat supply chain. Cooked chicken is a reasonable food source, not exceptional, but reliable enough that a small chicken farm covers early food needs without requiring any particularly sophisticated setup. Seeds breed them. Seeds are easy to find.

The egg-throwing mechanic deserves attention. Throw enough eggs and you'll occasionally spawn multiple chicks from a single throw — a two percent chance of four chicks at once. Large-scale chicken farms use this mechanic for rapid herd expansion: collect eggs, throw eggs, wait. The farm becomes self-sustaining and then overproducing and you end up with more chickens than you have any practical use for, which is a specific and comfortable kind of problem.

Baby Zombies sometimes ride Chickens. The Chicken Jockey is one of the rarer and more disorienting encounters in the game — a small fast hostile mob on the back of a passive one, moving at the speed of the zombie with the bobbing gait of the bird, appearing out of the dark and immediately closing distance. The Chicken in this scenario is blameless. It is a mount by circumstance rather than choice.

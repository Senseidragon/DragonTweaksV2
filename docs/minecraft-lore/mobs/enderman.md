# Enderman

The rules with Endermen are simple and mostly about your eyes.

Don't look at them directly. Looking directly at an Enderman is a provocation — not a threat, not an accident, a provocation, the way meeting someone's gaze across a room and holding it is a choice. The Enderman knows the difference. It opens its mouth, it shakes, it comes for you. Once that starts it doesn't stop until one of you is done.

They stand about three blocks tall, which puts them in the category of things that don't fit through standard doorways and can't follow you into small spaces. This is useful. A two-block-high ceiling is functionally an Enderman barrier. Water is also useful — they teleport away from it immediately and take damage from rain. A narrow building near water is close to an Enderman-proof perimeter.

They pick up blocks. Not randomly — they have preferences. Dirt, grass, sand, gravel, clay, flowers, mushrooms, pumpkins, TNT, cactus. They carry the block around and sometimes put it down somewhere else. The why of this is not explained and probably shouldn't be investigated too hard. It's one of those behaviors that's easier to note and move past than to make sense of.

The Eyes of Ender lead back to them, in a way — the eyes are crafted from ender pearls, which Endermen drop. To find the stronghold you throw the eyes and follow where they lead, which means the path to the end runs through killing enough Endermen to make the tools that find the door. They're the connective tissue between the overworld and what comes after it.

Teleportation at will. They use it offensively and defensively — closing distance when attacking, dodging projectiles, escaping water. You cannot reliably land a ranged shot on an Enderman that knows you're shooting at it. Melee is cleaner.

They came from somewhere else. That's not a guess — the End is clearly where they belong, the way the Nether belongs to its own creatures. They're in the overworld but they're not of it. The way they move, the sounds they make, the specific wrongness of their proportions: all of it reads as a thing that arrived here rather than a thing that grew here.

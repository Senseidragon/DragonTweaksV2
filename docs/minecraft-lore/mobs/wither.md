# Wither

You build the Wither. That's the important part. Nothing in the world spawns it — you construct it deliberately from soul sand and three Wither Skeleton skulls, and the moment the final skull is placed it begins.

The Wither starts small and invulnerable, swelling for several seconds, and then it explodes. The explosion is large. Build it somewhere with room for the explosion or lose whatever structure you built it near. Underground is common — less exposure, easier to contain. Underwater is occasionally used for the same reason.

It fires Wither Skulls. Two types: slow black skulls that can be deflected by a solid block, and fast blue skulls that destroy any block they hit regardless of blast resistance. The blue skulls pass through most cover. The Wither in an enclosed space turns that space into rubble with the blue skulls. This is why the underground approach requires careful thought — the Wither will destroy the tunnel you're fighting in.

It inflicts the Wither effect. A dark damage-over-time that turns hearts black and drains health continuously. Healing items don't display the health they're restoring while Withered. Milk clears it.

At half health it gains a barrier that makes it immune to projectiles. The final phase is melee only — you have to close distance and hit it directly, which means taking Wither Skull fire at close range. Beacons, totems, strength potions: the endgame kit that makes this manageable.

The Nether Star drops on death. One Nether Star, always. It goes into the beacon recipe, which produces the beacon effect: a beam of light visible across the landscape and status buffs — Speed, Haste, Strength, Regeneration — within range of the base it's built in. The Wither is the lock. The beacon is the key. You fight one to get the other.

Don't build it near anything you value.

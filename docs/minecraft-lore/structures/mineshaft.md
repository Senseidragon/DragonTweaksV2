# Mineshaft

Mineshafts are how you discover that someone was here before you.

They generate underground, branching corridors of oak planks and fences extending through the stone in roughly perpendicular patterns that would make sense if someone had been systematically working a vein. The wood is old — mossy, lit with torch sconces at intervals, some of the fences fallen or rotten-looking. The rails run through some corridors, with minecart chests sitting at intervals along the track, containing mining supplies and occasional valuables. The whole thing reads as an operation that was abandoned mid-work.

Cave Spider spawners appear in the shafts, usually in the corridors called "crossing rooms" where multiple passages meet. One spawner per crossing, sometimes two if the generation stacked them. The Cave Spiders are small enough to fit through one-block gaps, venomous, and they spawn from the block continuously if you're within activation range. The spawner is worth destroying — mine it out or place torches on every face to deactivate it — before attempting to loot the chests nearby, because looting chests while being continuously poisoned and hit is the mineshaft experience in its worst form.

String hangs across corridors from old tripwire setups. Not functional traps — just the remnants of whatever systems were here before. Breaking string produces the string item; leaving it means occasionally triggering nothing and feeling briefly alarmed.

The chests contain rails, torches, food, tools, and occasionally diamonds, golden apples, name tags, and enchanted books. The density of chests per mineshaft is high enough that a thorough clear of a large shaft system produces meaningful loot. Large shafts are generated randomly and some are small collections of corridors while others are enormous complexes that extend for hundreds of blocks.

The mineshaft connects to natural caves. They generate at the same depth ranges, their corridors cut through cave systems and are cut through by them in turn. A mineshaft corridor ending in open air above a cave is common. The transition between the two — built structure, then raw stone — clarifies what the mineshaft is: someone's attempt to systematically work what the caves produced naturally.

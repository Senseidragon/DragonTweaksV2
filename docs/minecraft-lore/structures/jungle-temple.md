# Jungle Temple

The Jungle Temple is older than the jungle. Or the jungle grew over it. The distinction matters less than the moss covering the cobblestone and the vines draping every exterior surface.

Mossy cobblestone construction, three floors, two traps, and a puzzle. The traps are tripwire — cords strung across corridors connected to dispensers loaded with arrows. Walk through without looking and you take a burst of arrows from the wall. The tripwire is visible if you're looking, invisible if you're moving fast through dense jungle having just spotted a structure and decided to explore immediately.

The puzzle is a lever mechanism on the ground floor. Three levers in a specific combination unlock a hidden chest in the wall rather than opening it directly. The combination isn't posted anywhere — you either work it out by experimenting or you break into the wall from outside, which bypasses the puzzle entirely and is the faster solution. The chest contains better loot than the main chest found through the stairs. Gold, emeralds, diamonds, enchanted books, horse armor — the distribution is similar to the desert temple.

Finding a Jungle Temple requires finding a jungle first, which is its own navigation challenge. Jungles aren't common and they're visually distinctive enough that spotting one from a distance is reliable, but reaching it through the jungle interior is dense work. The temples generate within the jungle, half-buried sometimes, overgrown always, positioned without regard for whether you can approach them easily.

Ocelots and parrots spawn nearby. Neither is hostile. The ocelot's wariness and the parrot's color against the temple's mossy stone are one of those ambient combinations that the world produces without trying.

The chest contents don't justify the trip the way a stronghold does. The Jungle Temple is an early-to-mid game destination — meaningful before you have better sources, less essential after.

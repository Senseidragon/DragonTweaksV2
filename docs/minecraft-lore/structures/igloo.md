# Igloo

The igloo sits on the snow and looks like shelter and mostly is.

Domed snow block construction, a single interior room with a bed, a furnace, a crafting table, a carpet on the floor. About half of all igloos have a trapdoor hidden under that carpet. The other half are what they appear to be — small refuges with basic amenities and nothing more.

The trapdoor leads down into a basement. The basement has a Zombie Villager in a cell, a Villager in another cell, a brewing stand with two Splash Potions of Weakness, a golden apple in a chest, and all the materials for a curing operation already assembled and waiting. The game has staged the cure. The Zombie Villager, the weakness potion, the golden apple — everything needed to perform the villager cure mechanic is present, in one place, with obvious purpose.

Whether this reads as a puzzle left by previous inhabitants, a coincidence of generation, or something the world arranged for you depends on how you want to think about it. The effect is the same: a free demonstration of one of the most economically valuable mechanics in the game, requiring only that you find the right igloo and notice the carpet.

The cured Villager trades at a permanent discount. Carried back to a settlement or used to seed a new trading post, a single igloo-basement Villager is worth more than most structures in the game in terms of long-term economic value.

The basement also has a ladder for getting back up, which is a small kindness. Igloos generate in snowy plains, snowy taigas, and snowy slopes. Finding one with a basement isn't guaranteed, but the possibility is reason enough to check every igloo you come across. Lift the carpet. Look for the trapdoor. The architecture of this world hides things in plain sight.

# Trial Chambers

Trial Chambers are the newest dungeon and the most mechanically distinct.

They generate underground in the deepslate layer, copper-and-tuff construction, procedurally assembled from a large pool of room templates. No two chambers are identical in layout — they're built from pieces that connect differently each generation, which means you can't memorize a floor plan. The navigation is part of the challenge.

The mechanic is the Trial Spawner. A new type of spawner that behaves differently from the standard variety: it detects how many players are present and scales the number of mobs it spawns to match. One player gets one mob at a time. Four players get four. The spawner tracks individual players and doesn't reset its challenge until each one either completes the trial or leaves. You can't cheese it by standing back and waiting — it knows you're there.

When a Trial Spawner's wave is defeated, it ejects loot. Not a chest — the spawner itself spits items into the room. Arrows, potions, emeralds, and the new items: wind charges, breeze rods, heavy core components toward the mace. The loot is automatic and proportional to the number of players who participated.

The Breeze is the new mob introduced with Trial Chambers. A wind-based hostile that fires wind charges — explosive projectiles that knock everything around them back — and deflects projectiles with its wind attacks. It's highly mobile, bouncing around the combat space, and its wind charges interact with the environment: they activate buttons and levers, open doors, move entities. The Breeze fights differently than anything else in the game.

Ominous Trial Chambers are the hard mode variant. Enter with Bad Omen active and the spawners become Ominous Spawners that produce harder mobs and drop better loot including Ominous Trial Keys and ultimately armor trims and the heavy core for crafting the mace.

The copper oxidizes. Older chambers have greener copper, newer ones are orange-fresh. Whether this happens in real time or is set at generation isn't immediately obvious from looking at it.

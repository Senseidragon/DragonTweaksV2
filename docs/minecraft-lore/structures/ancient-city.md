# Ancient City

They don't build them like this anymore — mostly because whatever built them isn't around to ask.

You find ancient cities the hard way: descending through the deepslate until the stone stops feeling like stone and starts feeling like *intention*. The corridors open up before you realize you've crossed from cave into architecture. Long wool-carpeted halls, blue and grey, swallowing your footsteps. Someone laid those carpets deliberately. Someone who knew that sound, down here, is a death sentence.

The scale is wrong in a way that takes a moment to register. These weren't built for a village. They were built for a *civilization* — one that apparently thrived in absolute darkness, beneath mountains, in the domain of something that cannot be reasoned with or outrun. The central frame of reinforced deepslate sits at the heart of it all, purpose unknown, and the longer you stare at it the less you want to know.

The architecture borrows from things you've seen elsewhere. A wing that echoes a pillager outpost. A corridor that feels like a woodland mansion hallway. Whether those surface builders borrowed from something older, or whether this is coincidence, I've stopped speculating. Neither answer is comforting.

Nothing lives here now. That's not the same as saying nothing is here.

The wool absorbs your movement. The darkness absorbs the light. And somewhere in the walls, the sculk is listening — not for you specifically, just for anything that dares to make a sound. You are not being hunted. You are simply being *reminded* that you are a guest in a place that has already decided what happens to loud guests.

Leave offerings. Leave quietly. Whatever was celebrated in these halls, the city remembers it still.

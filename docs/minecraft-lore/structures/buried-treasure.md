# Buried Treasure

The treasure map is the point of entry. Ocean ruins and shipwrecks contain maps marked with an X — a red X on a white background that corresponds to a location on the coast or underwater, and within a few blocks of that X, one block underground, is a chest.

The chest is always there. If the map says X, the chest is at X. This is reliable in a world where many things aren't. Dig at the X, usually on a beach, sometimes in shallow ocean floor, and you find it.

The contents: a Heart of the Sea, always — the unique item used to craft a Conduit, which underwater breathing and enhanced underwater vision when activated near a water structure. Gold and iron ingots. Cooked fish and cooked salmon. Emeralds. TNT. Prismarine crystals. Leather and iron armor. The Heart of the Sea is the reason you're here. Everything else is useful but secondary.

The Conduit built from the Heart of the Sea requires Nautilus Shells to complete — eight of them, one Heart of the Sea, forming a ring. Nautilus Shells come from Drowned or from fishing. The Conduit when placed underwater and surrounded by the right prismarine structure gives players in range the Conduit Power effect: underwater breathing, night vision underwater, and faster underwater mining. It converts an ocean base from an environment you're fighting against into one you can work in.

The map itself requires some navigation. The X is positioned relative to the map's display — centered on the X means you're at the right location. Maps in this world show terrain and player position; the treasure map specifically shows the X and your position relative to it, which makes navigation to the site mechanical rather than exploratory.

The beach where buried treasure appears is otherwise unremarkable. No structure above ground, no marker. Just sand, the coast, and something underneath worth finding.

# Pillager Outpost

See the watchtower first, always. That's the point — they want you to see it. A pillager outpost is a statement as much as a structure: *we are here, we are watching, and the village down the road knows it.*

They plant these things at the edges of settled land. Never inside a village, never far enough away to be ignorable — right at the frontier, where the farms thin out and the paths stop being maintained. The tower commands the high ground. Targets are set up in the yard, not for decoration. Tents and cages cluster around the base, and in those cages, more often than not, you'll find an iron golem — the kind the villagers built to protect themselves, now captured and penned up while its makers try not to notice the watchtower on the horizon.

The captain is who you're really there for. Distinct banner, distinct bearing, and carrying an ominous bottle that will make your life very complicated if you let it. Kill the captain and you inherit that complication — a bad omen that follows you into the nearest village and starts a raid. Do it anyway. The bottle has uses, and a captain left alive just breeds more of the same.

Pillagers respawn here. Clear it once and it fills again, slower but steadily. Some travelers set up camp nearby and farm the respawns for crossbow bolts and emeralds. Efficient, if you've got the patience and the armor for it.

What the pillagers actually *want* with the villages — beyond raiding, beyond the cages and the watchtowers — I've never quite worked out. They're organized enough to maintain supply lines. They're organized enough to coordinate raids. That's more than mindless aggression. Whatever they're building toward, the outpost is one piece of it.

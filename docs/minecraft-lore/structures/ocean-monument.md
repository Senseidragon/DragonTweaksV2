# Ocean Monument

You see the light before you see the structure. Deep water is dark, and then it isn't — a pale greenish glow rising from below, growing as you descend, resolving into columns and archways of prismarine that pulse with bioluminescence. Twenty-three pillars stretching from the monument floor down to the seabed. Someone built this to last.

The guardians make themselves known immediately. Hostile in a way that's more territorial than aggressive — this is their monument, their water, and you are an intrusion they are equipped to handle. The elder guardians are worse: three of them, one at the peak, one in each wing, ancient and enormous, radiating a curse that saps your mining strength the moment you enter their domain. Mining Fatigue. They don't want you breaking their walls, and they have the means to discourage it.

At the heart of the monument is the treasure chamber — eight blocks of gold encased in dark prismarine, inaccessible until the elder guardians are dead. Whoever built this place wanted their most valuable possession protected by the most powerful guardians. The architecture is designed around that room. Everything else is approach, defense, misdirection.

The sponge rooms are the oddity. Chambers accessible only from below, ceilings hung with wet sponges — natural? cultivated? No one I've asked has agreed. The monuments vary inside; no two layouts are identical, which means each one is a new navigation problem, a new set of wrong turns in airless corridors with a guardian around every corner.

Surface-dwellers sometimes wonder why anyone would build a civilization underwater. Spend enough time inside a monument and the question reverses itself. Up here it's raids and weather and everything that walks on two legs and wants what you have. Down there, the water keeps the riff-raff out. The guardians handle the rest.

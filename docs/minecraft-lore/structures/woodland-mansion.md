# Woodland Mansion

You'll walk for a long time before you find one. That's not an accident.

Woodland mansions rise out of dark oak forest like something that decided to become architecture instead of growing. Three storeys of cobblestone and dark timber, grand staircase visible through the upper windows, torchlight that somehow makes the exterior feel darker rather than warmer. They are large the way a threat is large — not impressive, exactly. *Imposing.*

The illagers inside have organized themselves into something that functions like a society, if you squint and don't think too hard about what they do with the allays locked in their lower rooms. Vindicators hold the ground floors, axes in hand, patrolling with the humorless efficiency of guards who expect trouble and are fine with that. The evokers occupy the upper chambers — older, stranger, surrounded by the residue of whatever passes for magic among their kind. They don't come to you. They wait.

The rooms tell stories if you look. Wool chambers in colors that don't match anything practical. A fake End portal — obsidian and frames but no eyes, whether an aspiration or a mockery I couldn't say. Hidden rooms that weren't meant to be found, sealed off behind unmarked walls, containing things that raise more questions than answers.

The imprisoned allays are what stays with me. Small, ghostly, musical creatures — locked in cages by things that seem to hate anything that isn't grim and angular. Whether they're prisoners, tools, or something else, the allays never stop humming even in captivity.

Clear the mansion if you have to. The loot justifies it and the captive allays deserve better. But don't mistake clearing it for understanding it. The illagers were doing something in there. Whether that something is finished or merely interrupted is not a question the empty halls answer.

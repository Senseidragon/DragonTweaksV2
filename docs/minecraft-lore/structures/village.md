# Village

The first time you crest a hill and see torch-light below — actual organized torch-light, not a lava spill or a burning tree — something in you relaxes that you didn't realize was clenched.

Villages are alive in a way most of the world isn't. Not sophisticated, not exactly, but *present*. Villagers shuffle between their beds and their workstations with the focused indifference of people who have things to do and no interest in your opinion about it. The blacksmith hammers. The farmer tends rows that somehow stay tended. The priest stands at his altar doing whatever priests do when no one is watching. An iron golem makes its slow circuit of the perimeter, and everything with teeth stays outside the light.

They'll trade with you. Emeralds are the currency — don't ask me why, that's just how it is. A villager who knows you'll keep coming back treats you better than one who's never seen your face. Reputation matters here. Saving the village from a raid earns you a Hero's welcome and discounts you'll still be spending months later.

Then there are the abandoned ones.

Same layout, same beds, same workstations — but the torches are out, the glass is dirty, cobwebs hang in the doorframes, and the only residents shuffle and moan. Zombie villagers. They wear the same robes as their living counterparts, same professions, same routes. If you're patient and well-supplied, you can cure them. Takes a golden apple and a splash potion of weakness, and a few minutes of listening to sounds you'd rather not hear. Some of them come back. Most do.

I've rebuilt two abandoned villages from scratch. Worth the effort every time. A living village is a landmark you can plan around. An empty one is just a reminder of what this world does to things left undefended.

# Desert Temple

The Desert Temple rises out of the sand looking like it was always there and the desert grew up around it.

Sandstone construction, orange terracotta patterning, a central tower with a blue-and-orange block at the peak that reads as a marker from a distance. Inside: a hollow interior chamber, four chests buried in the floor, and a pressure plate connected to nine blocks of TNT beneath the chests. This is the trap. Step on the plate or mine directly down to the chests without accounting for it and the explosion takes the chests, their contents, and you.

The correct approach is to mine or dig down along the wall of the chamber to reach the TNT level from the side, disable the pressure plate, and then open the chests normally. Or carefully mine to the chests from the side without touching the plate. The trap is not subtle — the pressure plate is visible on the floor — but in the excitement of finding the temple it's easy to move without thinking.

The loot is variable. Diamonds, emeralds, enchanted books, gold and iron ingots, horse armor, saddles. The range is wide enough that a temple can be disappointing or significant depending on what the generation decided. The enchanted book is the most consistent value.

Husks spawn in deserts and will be inside or near the temple at night. Husks are desert Zombies — same behavior, no sunburn, inflict Hunger on hit rather than just damage. They're not specific to the temple but they're often the ambient threat while you're looting it.

Archaeologists find suspicious sand inside desert temples through brushing — a new mechanic that uses a brush on suspicious-looking sand blocks to extract pottery sherds and small artifacts. The temple is a loot location and an archaeology site simultaneously, two different systems occupying the same structure.

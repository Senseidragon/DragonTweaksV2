# Stronghold

Every eye of ender you throw eventually stops climbing and drops. Follow enough of them and they'll lead you to a patch of ground that looks like every other patch of ground — until you dig.

Strongholds don't announce themselves. They sit beneath the world like a held breath, stone-bricked and labyrinthine, smelling of age and something faintly wrong. The spiral staircase at the entrance is always deliberate — whoever designed this place was thinking about movement, about ceremony. You descend into intersecting corridors that branch without logic, dead ends that used to be rooms, libraries with bookshelves still standing after what must be centuries.

Those libraries bother me most. Someone cared enough to stock them. Someone expected to return.

The infested stone is the other thing. It looks like ordinary stone until your pickaxe connects and something *moves* — silverfish, spilling out like the rock itself is alive and angry. The deeper you go, the more of it there is. Whether that's infestation spreading inward from the caves or something the original builders used intentionally, I've never settled on an answer.

At the end of every stronghold, past the monster spawner and the broken portcullis, is the room. Twelve frames of stone around a void, most of them empty. The eyes you carried down fit into those frames like they were always meant to be there — because they were. Whoever built this place built it around that portal. Built the libraries, the corridors, the staircase, all of it, as an approach to that single room.

Where the portal leads is its own education. What the builders were doing with it is a question the stronghold has never answered.

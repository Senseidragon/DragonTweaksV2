# Plains

Everyone starts in the plains, or close enough.

Open sky, gentle hills, grass running to the horizon in every direction. You can see far enough to plan, which is not something every biome lets you do. Tulips in clusters, cornflowers in the low spots, oxeye daisies scattered along the ridge lines. Oak trees standing alone or in small groups, some of them with bee nests tucked into the branches, the bees doing their patient rounds between the flowers. Horses in the distance, running for no reason in the way horses do.

Villages appear here more reliably than almost anywhere else — oak-framed, sensible, built by people who looked at flat land and said *yes, here.* The iron golem makes its circuit. The farmer tends the wheat. It's a comprehensible kind of life, a legible one, and after enough time in places where nothing is legible you learn to appreciate that.

The plains feel safe in a way that's worth naming carefully: they feel safe until dark. After dark the grass is exactly as good a hiding place as you'd expect. The open sightlines that help you during daylight work equally well for skeletons with arrows. Plains nights are hostile in the straightforward way that a lot of open terrain is hostile — you can see the threat coming and so can it see you, and there's nowhere to go except somewhere with a door.

Still. Plains are where things begin. Settlements, expeditions, the first tentative marks of a life somewhere. The horizon goes on, the sky is wide, and everything is possible in the way that only flat empty space makes possible — before you've started making choices and the choices start closing down.

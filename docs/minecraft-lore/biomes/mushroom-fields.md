# Mushroom Fields

You find mushroom fields by accident, usually. You're sailing far enough from everything that the compass stops feeling useful, and then the horizon does something wrong — the colors are off, the sky is yellower than it should be, and as you get closer you realize the islands ahead are covered not in grass but in something purple and alive.

Mycelium. The ground itself is fungal here.

The mushrooms are enormous, red and brown, taller than most trees. Mooshrooms wander between them — cows, more or less, except they're red and spotted with white and have mushrooms growing out of their backs, which raises questions I've stopped asking. They're peaceful. Everything here is peaceful. That's the part that takes getting used to.

No creepers. No skeletons. Nothing with intent. Even underground the usual threat is absent — dig down in a mushroom field and whatever hunts in normal caves apparently doesn't follow. I've never been able to explain this to anyone's satisfaction, including my own. The mycelium reaches the surface and something about that seems to hold the dark at bay.

The sky at dusk goes red in a way that looks like fire and isn't. At night it dims to a color that's almost purple. The place looks like it belongs somewhere else — another world, maybe, or a dream someone had about this one.

I've never built anything in mushroom fields. It feels wrong, somehow. Like setting up camp in a cathedral. You visit. You take nothing. You leave it the way you found it.

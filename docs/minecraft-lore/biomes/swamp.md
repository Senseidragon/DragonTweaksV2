# Swamp

The water is the wrong color. Not dangerously wrong, not a warning — just gray-green, murky, shot through with underwater fog that limits visibility to arm's length. You wade into it and the world above stays visible but strange, the surface broken by lily pads and the occasional squid. The floor is mud and it pulls at your feet in the way mud does.

Swamp oaks grow bent and heavy with vines. Not the clean, upward growth of forest oaks — these trees lean, branch sideways, hang close to the water's surface with a posture that suggests decades of compromise with gravity and wet soil. Blue orchids grow at the waterline, the only biome where you'll find them. Everything else is brown and green and gray. The orchids stand out like someone left something colorful behind by accident.

Witch huts appear in swamps. Small structures on stilts over the water, a black cat inside, a witch inside with the cat. Whether the huts came before the witches or the witches chose the swamps for their huts is a question with no good answer. The witch herself — hunched, robed, perpetually irritated by your presence — is less a monster than a neighbor you'll never get along with. She throws potions. You try to stay out of range. Everyone manages.

At night, with the right moon, slimes emerge from the muddy ground. They don't feel like natural creatures — too geometric, too simple, bouncing with an enthusiasm that seems disconnected from anything a living thing would feel about being a living thing. They're the swamp's particular contribution to the catalog of inexplicable things.

It's not a comfortable biome. It's not meant to be. But the blue orchids are worth the wading.

# Snowy Plains

Cold has a color here and the color is blue.

Not the comfortable blue of sky or water — something icier than that, a color that the light takes on when it passes through enough snow. The snowy plains in morning look like the world is made of something fragile, every surface crystalline, the horizon sharp against a sky that hasn't warmed up yet. By midday it's merely white and bright. By evening it's blue again, and colder.

Water freezes almost as soon as it's exposed. Rivers freeze over. Small ponds freeze completely. Anything that involves flowing water becomes a project in keeping it from becoming ice, which is not a project the snowy plains care about helping you with. The cold is not hostile in the way that hostile means a monster with intent — it's just what this place is, indifferent to your requirements, going to keep being cold after you leave.

Rabbits and polar bears. Those are your neighbors here. The rabbits are quick and cautious. The polar bears are calm unless you get between a parent and its cub, at which point they become a reminder that peaceful mob does not mean safe mob. They move across the snow with a weight and economy that suggests they know exactly what they're doing, which is more than can be said for most visitors.

Igloos appear sometimes — small, domed, a trapdoor in the floor that leads to something unexpected. Spruce villages, low to the ground, roofed against snow. Pillager outposts with snow on the watchtower roof, indistinguishable from their warmer counterparts except for the setting.

The snowy plains are not comfortable. They're clarifying. Everything that doesn't need to be there has been stripped away by the cold, and what remains is just the world, undecorated, working on its own terms.

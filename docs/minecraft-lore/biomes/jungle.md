# Jungle

The canopy closes over you and the sky disappears.

Jungle trees grow tall enough that you forget there's sky. Two-by-two trunks, thirty blocks high, branching into a ceiling of leaves so dense that the ground beneath stays in permanent half-light. Vines hang from everything. Ferns cover everything else. You move by machete logic — constantly clearing, constantly re-orienting, constantly aware that the temple you're looking for could be twenty blocks away and invisible.

That's the thing about jungle temples. They're not small. They're stone pyramids with trap mechanisms and hidden rooms, and the jungle swallows them whole regardless. You find them by accident, usually, when your sword connects with something that isn't a vine. The jungle doesn't hide its structures out of malice; it simply grows without reference to what was there first.

Parrots live here and nowhere else worth mentioning. They mimic sounds, which in a jungle full of sounds means you're never entirely sure what's real. An ocelot won't let you approach it directly — you earn their company by being patient and small and unthreatening, which is not a natural state for someone who's been carving through vegetation for the last half hour. Pandas exist here in the same unreachable way that most remarkable things exist here: present, visible, indifferent to you specifically.

The orange fog sits low in the evenings. The night sky is brighter than it has any right to be, blue and vivid through breaks in the canopy. The jungle is alive in a way that implies the living is not for your benefit — you are passing through something ongoing, something that was here before and will be here after, and the best you can do is not break too much of it on your way.

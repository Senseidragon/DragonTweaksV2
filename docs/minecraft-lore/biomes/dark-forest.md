# Dark Forest

The problem with the dark forest is not the darkness. You get used to darkness. The problem is that the darkness is *above* you.

Dark oak grows tight and tall, and the canopy those trees form together blocks out the sky so completely that hostile mobs can spawn on the forest floor in full daylight. You emerge into what should be a bright afternoon and immediately have a skeleton to deal with. The forest doesn't care what time it is. The rules about sunlight and safety don't apply here because the canopy makes its own night, and the mobs have noticed.

Giant mushrooms push up between the trees — red and brown, adding another layer to the permanent twilight underneath. The forest floor is a maze of trunks and mushroom stalks and leaf litter, visibility limited to whatever your torch can reach and not much beyond. Wolves don't live here, which I've always found telling. The wolves that den in ordinary forests have apparently decided this one isn't for them.

Somewhere in the dark forest, at the end of a very long walk, is the mansion. You won't find it without a map unless you get extraordinarily lucky — these woods are vast and the mansion is just another dark shape in a biome of dark shapes until you're close enough to see the windows. The evokers inside are patient. They have no particular reason to hurry.

I light torches obsessively in dark forests. More than I need. More than makes sense. There's something about the inverted logic of the place — that sunlight doesn't help, that the danger comes from above — that makes me want more light than I can carry, just to feel like the rules still work the way I expect them to.

# Deep Dark

The sculk doesn't glow so much as it *doesn't not glow* — a faint blue-green that your eyes chase in the darkness without ever quite finding. The deeper you go, the more of it there is: sculk spreading across the stone like a slow tide, roots and tendrils and sensors and the occasional shrieker sitting patient and terrible in the middle of a corridor.

The deep dark is not a cave. Caves have logic — they follow water and erosion and the grain of the stone. The deep dark has the sculk, and the sculk has its own geometry, its own growth, its own agenda that it isn't sharing. It covers the floor and climbs the walls and occasionally gives way to an ancient city, rising out of the fungal blue-black like a monument to something that no longer needs monuments.

Sound is the thing you have to understand about the deep dark. The sculk sensors listen. Every footstep, every block broken, every chest opened — the vibrations travel through the stone and through the sculk and the shriekers count. Three shrieks and the patrol ends. Whatever comes next doesn't patrol. It hunts.

I've been in the deep dark enough times to have opinions about it, which means I've been lucky enough times to have survived long enough to form them. Go in crouching. Don't mine. Don't fight anything. Take only what you can reach without making noise, and leave the rest. The ancient cities have things worth having, but the deep dark charges full price for them and negotiates nothing.

The sculk is still spreading. Somewhere below every mountain, it grows a little further every day. Whether toward anything in particular, I couldn't say. Whether the direction matters, I'm not sure I want to know.

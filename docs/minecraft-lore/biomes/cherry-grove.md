# Cherry Grove

There's a moment when you crest the wrong ridge and suddenly everything is pink.

Not the violent pink of a sunset or the sickly pink of a potion effect — something quieter than that. Cherry trees in full bloom, petals drifting sideways in air that isn't quite moving, a canopy that turns ordinary afternoon light into something rosy and soft. The first time it happened to me I stood at the ridge for longer than I care to admit.

Cherry groves tuck themselves into mountain ranges, which means you earn them. They don't spread across plains or colonize valleys — they perch on slopes and plateaus and the tucked-away places that take work to reach. The approach is usually through something much less forgiving: stone, ice, bare rock. And then the trees start, and the petals start, and you're somewhere else entirely.

Bees nest in the trunks here in numbers you don't see elsewhere. The groves hum. It's not threatening — it's the hum of a place that's working, that has its own purposes and doesn't need yours. The bees move between the blossoms with the single-minded efficiency of creatures that have never doubted what they're supposed to be doing.

Nobody builds permanent settlements in cherry groves. Whether that's because the terrain doesn't suit it or because no one can bring themselves to cut down enough of the trees, I've never determined. Pillager outposts appear sometimes — finding one in a grove feels like discovering something ugly tucked inside something beautiful, which is a feeling the pillagers seem to inspire everywhere they set up shop.

I take the petals when I leave. Pack them carefully. They don't last — they're decorative and fragile and impractical. I take them anyway.
